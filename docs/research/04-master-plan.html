⚠ The one rule that applies to everything below

The free law data has real, admitted flaws — some old Supreme Court judgments don't show up when you filter by date, and every High Court judgment (including all of Karnataka's) has no working "which other cases quote this one" link, only the Supreme Court does. None of this makes the data unusable. It does mean: anything any of these tools finds needs a human to check it against the real source before relying on it — this is a law firm's name attached to the answer, not a hobby project. That's not a stage to grow out of, it's a permanent rule, same as Mitra never giving advice on someone's specific case.

The plan, in order

Three separate things — Mitra (free, public), a search tool (private, for us), a drafting tool (private, for us) — each only makes sense once the step before it is working.

  • Step 1

    Finish polishing Mitra as it exists today

    Faster responses, real spam protection, a "was this helpful" button, merging duplicated code. Already underway.

  • Step 2

    Give Mitra the free law library instead of a handwritten cheat-sheet refined

    Swap hand-typed answers for real Karnataka High Court judgments and real law text. Two further upgrades once that's working: teach Mitra to search again if its first attempt wasn't enough instead of always answering off one lookup, and use the specialized Indian-legal-language model — correctly, this time — to sort a question into the right practice area before searching, which it's actually built for.

  • Step 3

    Build a private search tool, just for the firm now concrete

    A "Google, but only for Indian case law" tool for your own research. Store the data on your own computer, index it with a free, simple search method, and connect it to Claude directly — meaning you can search it right inside a conversation like this one, no extra app, no extra login, no extra AI subscription needed for the "search" part.

    Why separate from Mitra? Mitra talks to the public and has to stay cautious. An internal tool is just for you, so it can go deeper without that caution.
  • Step 4

    Build a drafting / contract-review tool one fix applied

    Adapt the existing free Word add-in mentioned above — a working, mature tool already, not a demo. One important fix: give it its own free Gemini key, separate from Mitra's — checked Mitra's actual settings and it's already using more than half its speed allowance alone, so sharing one key between the two risks both slowing down or failing together.

  • Step 5

    Measure whether any of this is actually working

    A set of test questions with known correct answers, graded automatically — there's a working scoring method to copy for this too, not just something to invent from scratch.

  • Later, if wanted

    Sell the search and drafting tools to other small firms

    Both become sellable mostly by wrapping the same working backend in a simple screen for other people to use — not a rebuild. Being honest about the other half: this makes the firm partly a software vendor to other firms, which is ongoing work (support, keeping it running for someone else), not a one-time thing. A real comparable Indian product charges ₹9,000–17,250 per person per month — real room to charge less, given our costs are near zero, but only worth it if taking on that ongoing responsibility is actually wanted.

What your GPU actually adds

A GPU (graphics card) is the part of a computer built for doing huge amounts of math at once — originally for video games, but that same ability is exactly what AI needs. Yours (RTX 3060, 6GB) is a real, useful one — not top-of-the-line, but genuinely capable for the right jobs.

What it's good for

  • Powering the search tool's "understand meaning, not just keywords" upgrade, for free. Turning case text into something searchable by meaning normally means paying a cloud AI service per document, even if only a small fee. Your GPU can do that exact conversion locally instead — for the entire scoped slice of Karnataka High Court cases and law text, for free, with no speed limit and no ongoing bill. This is a real upgrade over the plan's earlier assumption of using a cloud AI service's free tier for this step — genuinely better, now that the GPU is properly accounted for.
  • Teaching Mitra your own writing, for free. The free law data covers public law, not the firm's own past blog posts, opinions, or way of explaining things. Your GPU can convert your own documents into something searchable the same way, as many times as you want, at no cost.
  • A free practice space. While building and testing any of this, every real test question normally costs a small amount through Gemini. Your GPU can run a smaller, free AI model locally for rough testing during development — not as good as Gemini, but good enough to catch bugs before spending real calls confirming things properly.

What it's not for

It can't run the actual live chatbot that visitors talk to. A laptop gets closed, goes to sleep, and isn't always connected — the real website needs to be reachable 24/7 from anywhere, which needs a proper always-on server, same as Mitra already runs on today. Think of the GPU as a workshop, not the storefront.

Other real ideas worth having on the list

Judge conduct & argument-style lookup verified legitimate

Not "predict how a judge will rule" — narrower and genuinely useful: surface what style or tone of argument a specific judge has previously pushed back on, so you know what to avoid before appearing in front of them. Built from two text sources: judgments themselves, where judges sometimes explicitly note conduct in their orders, and legal-journalism coverage (LiveLaw, Bar & Bench regularly quote a judge's real-time oral remarks, not just the final written order). The video/facial-analysis version of this same idea is off the table — checked directly against the Supreme Court's own July 24, 2026 order, which bans third parties from downloading, storing, or reusing hearing recordings without permission (only the Court's own official stream is exempt). Recognized news reporting is explicitly still allowed, which is exactly why the text-based route above holds up as the real path, not a workaround.

Track what people actually ask Mitra

A simple, free log of the real questions visitors type in. Right now, which topics to prioritize for the knowledge base is a guess — this would tell us for certain.

Build free first, pay only once something's proven

Prototype the search and drafting tools using free local tools and your GPU first, confirm they're genuinely useful, and only move to paid always-on hosting once it's clear it's worth paying for — rather than committing to a monthly bill from day one.

What's deliberately not on this plan

Being honest about the ceiling matters as much as the plan itself.

Training our own AI model from scratch. Would cost more than the entire rest of this plan combined, for a worse result than just using Gemini and Claude well.
Fine-tuning a model on your GPU. Technically possible in a very limited way, but 6GB is right at the edge of what that needs — not worth the time for what little it would improve.
Running the live chatbot off your laptop. Covered above — a laptop can't be the thing 24/7 website visitors rely on.
Reusing the free dataset's own pre-computed "search by meaning" index directly. It's built with a specific paid AI model — using it means paying that provider per search. Re-doing this ourselves on the GPU avoids that entirely, for our own slice of the data.
Mitra giving case-specific legal advice to visitors. A firm rule, not a technical one — doesn't change no matter how good any of this gets.

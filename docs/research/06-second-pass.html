01

What's actually in there

Four things I missed entirely, all Apache-2.0, all directly usable.

scripts/citations/indian_formats.py

Best find

A complete Indian legal citation parser. Every major reporter: AIR, SCC, SCC OnLine (Supreme and High Court), SCR, MANU, SCALE, JT, ILR, Cri LJ, Comp Cas, ITR, Taxman, GST, ITAT, NCLT/NCLAT — plus state reporters (Bom LR, DLT, KLJ, KLT, MLW, ALJ, PLR, CWN, Guj LR). It handles the new 2024–25 neutral citation formats including bench indicators (2024:AHC-LKO:12762, -DB suffixes), and ships four separate court-code lookup tables mapping AIR, neutral, SCC Online and MANU codes to court names.

Use it for: validating every citation in a draft before it leaves the office — does this citation parse, and does the court code match the court named? That's a whole class of error caught mechanically. Also: extracting the citation graph from the firm's own past documents.

scripts/release/redaction.py

Solves a real blocker

Indian-specific PII redaction, and it's carefully built rather than naive. Phone, email, and then PAN / IFSC / Aadhaar each requiring a context word nearby — because Indian case numbers share those shapes (their example: WPCT0123456 is an IFSC by shape, ABCDE1234F is a PAN by shape, and masking on shape alone corrupts the judgment). Aadhaar candidates are additionally checked against the Verhoeff checksum. It also redacts named relatives of victims and named protected children (BNS s.72, JJ Act s.74 territory), and detects in-camera and non-publication directions.

Use it for: the drafting tool's actual blocker. To use the firm's own past documents as templates, they must be redacted first — and this does it, for Indian identifiers, with real care about false positives. This is the single most immediately useful file in the repository for us.

scripts/pipeline/UNIFIED_CHUNKING_SCHEMA.md

Changes search quality

Every chunk carries judges, disposition, bench_strength, acts_referenced, cases_cited, cited_by_count — and critically a section_type with a priority score across 15 categories: ratio_decidendi (100), conclusion (95), judgment (90), analysis (85), issues (80), obiter_dicta (75), arguments (70), facts (65), precedents (60), relief (55), statutes (50), procedure (45).

That distinction matters more than it looks. A generic RAG system retrieves whatever paragraph is textually similar — which is as likely to be a party's rejected submission or passing obiter as the binding holding. Being able to retrieve ratio preferentially is a genuinely legal-domain-aware retrieval feature, and most legal chatbots don't have it.

Use it for: Mitra's biggest quality upgrade — weight retrieval toward the binding reasoning, and be able to say "this is what the court held" versus "this is what a party argued."

legal_section_detector.py & extract_judges_dispositions.py

Confirms an earlier idea

The section detector is the 15-category classifier itself — regex plus heuristics, tuned for Supreme Court, High Court and tribunal document structures. Reusable on the firm's own documents to auto-structure them.

And the judges/dispositions extractor confirms that judges and disposition are real, populated fields — which means the judge argument-style lookup idea from the earlier plan is supported by data that already exists, not speculative. Not the whole tool, but the foundation is real.

The two corpora nobody is using

Their words, not mine

Flagged in their own audit as unused: parliament-debates — 12,317 text files, 6.4 GB, holding parliamentary debates, the Constitution, Law Commission reports, the gazette, IPC material and AIR volumes. Fully text-bearing, never indexed by anyone.

Use it for: legislative intent — why a provision reads the way it does, not just what it says. A different register of answer than section text, and nobody is using it.

03

Traps worth knowing before building

"In force" is wrong by 9.4% for Central legislation

Would mislead a client

From taxonomy.py: act_status has a fourth value, superseded (57,458 provisions), that naive code buckets as in-force. Vaquill note their own published spreadsheet does exactly this — reporting 613,970 Central provisions in force when 556,512 genuinely are.

These are mostly superseded RBI master directions and similar. If Mitra or a research tool told a client a superseded RBI direction was current, that is a real problem, not a cosmetic one. Any build on the legislation data must treat superseded as not-in-force explicitly.

Tribunal enrichment columns are empty

Thinner than the schema implies

headnotes, outcome, subject_matter, cited_acts and cited_sections are defined on every tribunal table and empty on all 813,168 rows. judges is populated only for CAT, DRT, TDSAT and RERA — not for ITAT, NCLT, CESTAT or GST AAR, which are the ones this firm cares about. NCLT is weakest: only 36,080 of 63,487 matters carry a PDF link.

A leaked third-party key in the repo

Don't depend on it

pdf_highlighter.py has a hardcoded PyMuPDF Pro trial API key committed into it. It isn't ours to use and it's a trial. The highlighting technique is fine to learn from; the file as written is not something to build on.

04

What the firm can actually do

Internal — safe, free, no conduct question

  • Limitation calculator — still the best first build. Deterministic, no AI, no Rule 36 exposure.
  • Own work product, redacted and indexed — now genuinely unblocked, because redaction.py handles the Indian PII problem properly. Redact past drafts and opinions, index on the GPU, and the firm has an assistant grounded in its own voice.
  • Citation validator — run every draft's citations through indian_formats.py before filing. Small, mechanical, catches real errors.
  • Karnataka HC research index — 581K judgments, in one collection, no dedup needed, with ratio-versus-obiter retrieval.
  • Judge and disposition analytics — from fields that already exist in the data.
  • Legislative-intent lookup — the parliament corpus nobody is using.
  • Conflict checking and cause-list monitoring — as before, and try the official eCourts app first.

Public-facing — narrow, and only one thing qualifies

Under Rule 36 as covered last time, nothing built on this data should be public if its purpose or effect is bringing in work. The one channel that is expressly permitted is Rule 51: legal writing, editing law texts, teaching, journalism.

So: genuine scholarship — an article on what the corpus shows about, say, disposal patterns in Karnataka IP matters — is permitted territory in a way a branded search utility is not. The distinction is real and worth respecting rather than working around.

Commercial, with someone else running it

You asked specifically about doing this with someone's help, and that is the shape the rules point toward — though I'd want you to confirm it rather than take my reading.

Rule 47 bars an advocate from personally engaging in business, but expressly allows being a sleeping partner in a firm doing business — subject to the State Bar Council forming the view that the business is not inconsistent with the dignity of the profession. Rule 48 separately permits a non-executive directorship, while barring Managing Director and Secretary roles.

So the structure that appears open: a separate entity, genuinely run day-to-day by someone who is not you — a technical co-founder or operator — with you passive, and a State Bar Council view taken before anything is built or sold, not after. What is not open is you building it, running it, and selling it yourself.

I'm reading rules here, not advising you — you are the advocate and this is your enrolment. But it does mean "with someone's help" isn't a workaround; it's closer to the actual permitted form.

05

How far Mitra can be pushed

Revised with what this pass found. The last two rungs are new.

  • Now

    Finish the polish already underway — streaming, real rate limiting, feedback capture.

  • Next

    Real law instead of hand-typed answers — Karnataka HC and Central legislation, with superseded handled correctly.

    That last clause is not optional; see the traps above.

  • Then

    Agentic retrieval — search again when the first attempt wasn't enough, rather than always answering off one lookup.

  • New

    Ratio-aware retrieval. Weight toward ratio_decidendi and conclusion chunks over arguments and facts, so Mitra quotes what a court held rather than what a party argued.

    This is the single biggest answer-quality upgrade available, and it exists because the data already carries section types.

  • New

    Mechanical citation validation. Before any answer is shown, run every citation through the parser: does it parse, does the court code match the court named, is the year plausible. Anything that fails gets dropped rather than displayed.

    This kills a whole category of hallucination without needing a better model — the most common and most damaging failure mode for legal AI, solved by regex.

  • Later

    Real benchmarking, practice-area routing, multilingual.

And the ceiling that doesn't move

None of this changes what Mitra is allowed to be. It still explains rather than advises, still refuses case-specific questions, and the open question from last time still stands — whether appending the firm's contact details to every answer is the right side of Rule 36. Better retrieval makes the explaining sharper; it does not buy any more latitude.
